At the end of this guide you have your [[empire]] set up, your prices on your home exchange, and a first saved [[plan]] whose profit and payback you can read. It's for players who are new to PRUNplanner, and it takes about 15 minutes. You need an account; a new account already comes with an empire and an [[exchange-preference|exchange preference]], so you mostly check and adjust.

## 1. Check your empire {#step-1}

Open [Empire](/empire). A new account has one empire, "My Empire", with no [[faction]] and 1 of 2 permits used. While it has no plans, the page shows three steps; the first, **Your empire**, holds its settings.

1. Pick your **Faction**. It gives the buildings of its [[expertise]] a bonus in every plan of the empire.
2. Set **Permits Total** and **Permits Used** as your in-game HQ shows them. Fewer used permits give a larger faction bonus.
3. Press **Save**.

What good looks like: the step shows **Saved**. Once the empire has plans, the same fields sit in the **Configuration** box of the Empire page.

> [!tip]
> Want a second empire, for example to test an expansion without touching your real bases? Create it in [Management](/manage) with **New Empire**.

More: [Empire configuration](/help/empire#configuration), [Faction bonus](/help/plan_base#faction).

## 2. Set your home exchange {#step-2}

Your prices come from your exchange preference. A new account has "My Exchange Preference", already used by "My Empire", set to **Universe · VWAP 30D**: the 30-day average across all four exchanges. Most players buy and sell on one home exchange instead.

1. Open [Exchanges](/exchanges). It opens your preference; **Switch Preference** picks another one if you have several.
2. In the list on the left, open **All planets**.
3. In the **Exchange price** box, under **How buy and sell prices are set**, keep **Same for buy and sell** and pick your home exchange: AI1 at Antares Station, CI1 at Benten, IC1 at Hortus or NC1 at Moria.
4. Pick a price basis, or click the preset **Average · VWAP 30D**.
5. Press **Save changes** in the bar at the bottom.

What good looks like: the **Overview** shows the new rule under **All planets**. Once you have plans, it lists every price they use, and amber prices mark materials that count as 0 ȼ.

More: [Set up your prices](/help/guide_cx_prices), [Setting an exchange rule](/help/exchanges#exchange-rule).

## 3. Find a planet {#step-3}

Open [Planet Search](/search).

1. Under Materials, use **+ add material…** to add what you want to make or extract. Each option shows how many planets you'd get.
2. Under **Distance**, tick your home exchange under **Exchanges** and lower **Max jumps**, so supply flights stay short.
3. Turn on **Plots** under **Columns**, so you see which planets still have free [[plots]].

What good looks like: a short list with the material's tile in green or yellow (close to the best planet's output), few jumps and free plots. A red ⊘ next to a name means the planet is full.

More: [Find a planet for a material](/help/guide_find_planet), [Planet Search](/help/planet_search#materials).

## 4. Create the plan {#step-4}

Click "+" on the planet's row. A new plan for that planet opens in a new tab, so your search stays.

1. **Empire & prices**: **Calculate with empire** shows the empire the plan calculates with; a new plan starts with your first empire. When you create the plan, it joins that empire. **Prices** names the exchange preference it uses.
2. **Planet**: the **COGC** select starts with the program the planet runs now. Leave **Permits** at 1 for a first base (500 area).
3. Production: pick a building in the building select, then click **+ Recipe** and pick a [[recipe]]. Set **Count** to how many you build. For extraction, click a resource tile under **Planet** instead; it adds the extractor and the recipe.
4. **Infrastructure**: leave **Auto** on. It picks the cheapest habitations that house all your workers.

> [!tip]
> On a planet many players plan on, an empty plan offers a card "Start from a typical setup on …": the buildings and experts others use there. It shows what others plan, not what earns most, so check the numbers before you keep it.

More: [Base setup](/help/plan_base), [Production & recipes](/help/plan_production#adding-buildings).

## 5. Read the results {#step-5}

The tiles at the top change with every edit.

| Tile | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| **Profit / day** | Positive. It is revenue minus bought materials, [[degradation]] and [[production-fees\|production fees]]. |
| **Payback** | Days until the base has paid for itself. "never" means it makes no profit. |
| **Workforce** | Red means a tier has fewer beds than workers. |
| **Area** | The used figure turns red when the base needs more area than your permits give. |
| **Storage** | Days until the storage is full, so how often a ship must come. |

[[material-io|Material I/O]] on the right lists what the base makes and uses per day: a positive Δ is sold, a negative one bought. A flag after a material means the market may not take that amount; click it to open Plan check.

> [!tip]
> Try one change at a time and watch **Profit / day**: another recipe, one more building, or a luxury turned off in **Workforce**. **Undo** (Ctrl+Z) takes a change back.

More: [Results](/help/plan_results), [Plan check](/help/plan_check#market-check).

## 6. Save the plan {#step-6}

1. Click the pencil next to the plan's name and type a name.
2. Press **Create** (or Ctrl+S, Cmd+S on a Mac).

What good looks like: the state reads "Saved …", and the plan is listed under **Plans** in the side menu when the menu shows its empire. From now on every save keeps a version, and unsaved changes are kept as a draft, so a closed tab loses nothing.

More: [Saving & versions](/help/plan_saving).
